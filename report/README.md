## Pareto frontiers

Each point is an entry point, labelled. The x-axis is hardware cycles at `native` (what rule 1 reads); the y-axis is the trade-off named in the title. Lower and further left is better on both. **Blue**, joined by a step line: the frontier of the two axes shown, among every measured entry: no other entry is better on one without being worse on the other, by more than ε = 5 % or, on cycles, the A/A band, and a cycles advantage callgrind's count contradicts does not count. **Orange ring**: on the frontier the verdict reads at this workload, which weighs all six axes (the four decode-side ones for a decode-only entry): at a Kynos workload rule 1's, where an entry is ringed if none of the eligible entries that have not failed conformance dominates it; at any other, the *deserves to exist* frontier, where none of the entries that have not failed conformance does. An entry that fails conformance can be ringed: it is weighed against that pool, but cannot dominate it. **Hollow**: the entry fails conformance. The gray crosshair is serde_json. Axes are logarithmic where the figures span 10× or more. Each SVG carries the figures as hover titles.

<a id="limitations"></a>

## Limitations

- One host: every host-measured number is from AMD Ryzen 7 7800X3D 8-Core Processor. No Intel CPU is measured.
- Hardware cycles is `solo` class: taken on a shared machine (1, 5 and 15 minute load average 2.37 2.00 1.95 as it finished; governor powersave), not a quieted one. Its A/A band says how much of a difference is noise.
- Timed sampling is `solo` class: taken on a shared machine (1, 5 and 15 minute load average 2.21 2.38 2.38 as it finished; governor powersave), not a quieted one. Its A/A band says how much of a difference is noise.
- End-to-end timing is `solo` class: taken on a shared machine, not a quieted one. Its A/A band says how much of a difference is noise.
- Run lengths: hardware cycles 3 rounds; timed sampling 3 rounds; end to end 5 reps of 30 s per load phase; compile time 5 builds; fuzzing 1800 s per target and configuration.
